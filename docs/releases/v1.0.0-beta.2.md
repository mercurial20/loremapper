# Loremapper v1.0.0-beta.2

Worlds that make sense, and a way to measure them.

**Try it online:** <https://mercurial20.github.io/loremapper/>. Nothing to install, and your maps stay in your browser.

> Still a **beta**. Please export a backup (`Export → Editable project`) of maps
> you care about. **Seeds from beta.1 now build different worlds.**

## A new world generator

Click **Generate** and pick a world type or template. Each one now really
produces what it says.

- **Plate tectonics:**
  - Continents ride on plates.
  - Mountain ranges, volcanic island arcs and deep trenches rise where plates collide.
  - Mid-ocean ridges and rift seas open where plates pull apart.
  - Old, worn-down ranges cross the interiors.
- **World types you can count on.** *Continents* are separate, a *Supercontinent* is one landmass, and an *Ocean world* has only islands. There are new templates too: *Old & New World*, *Inner sea*, *Polar continent*, *Shattered continent* and *Mainland & isles*.
- **Plains are flat.** Most land is lowland, steppe or plateau, and hills and peaks gather in mountain belts. Close-ups are no longer covered in noise.
- **Climate and water:**
  - prevailing winds bring rain to windward coasts and leave rain shadows behind mountains;
  - rivers drain real basins, have tributaries, and always run downhill in their own valleys;
  - closed basins fill into flat river plains.
- **Biomes where they belong:**
  - desert, steppe, grassland and forest follow moisture and heat;
  - tundra and ice follow the cold;
  - swamps form in warm wet lowlands;
  - farmland lies along rivers and coasts;
  - a great river keeps a green strip through the desert;
  - **steppe** is a new biome you can paint (key `8`).
- **Settlements where people would build them.** Towns sit on rivers and coasts, castles on hills, watchtowers in passes.
- **Realism levels:**

  | Level | Time | What it adds |
  |---|---|---|
  | Easy | ≈ 2 s | |
  | Medium | ≈ 3 s | light erosion |
  | High | ≈ 6 s | valleys |
  | Ultra | ≈ 10 s | fine valleys at full resolution |

  The generation can be cancelled while it runs.
- **A redesigned dialog:**
  - a live preview of exactly what you'll get;
  - six seed variants;
  - advanced settings for land share, mountains, hills, temperature, rainfall, rivers and settlements.

## How big is it?

- With the Select tool (`V`), click any land or water. It's outlined, and the panel shows:
  - its **true area on the sphere** and its share of all land;
  - its coastline and its highest or deepest point;
  - its extent in latitude and longitude, and the realms on it.
- Name it, and write the name on the map as a label.
- **Lands & seas** lists every continent, island, sea and lake by size.
- Territories show how much land lies inside their border.
- Switch between metric (km², km, m) and imperial (mi², mi, ft).

## Also new since beta.1

- The online version on GitHub Pages is deployed on every push.
- Search and link-preview metadata, so shared links show a proper card.
- Privacy-friendly Cloudflare Web Analytics, **on the official site only**. Docker, npm and self-hosted copies have none, and maps never leave your browser.

## Upgrading

- **Docker:** `git pull && docker compose up -d --build`.
- **Node.js:** `git pull && npm ci && npm run build && npm run preview`.

Your maps live in the browser, so updating doesn't touch them.

## Tested

- Lint, type-check, 31 unit tests and the production build pass.
- Automated runs in Chrome 154, Firefox 157 and WebKit 27.2 (macOS) covered the full editor workflow:
  - every world type and realism level;
  - cancelling a generation;
  - island-in-view;
  - measuring continents and territories.

  They found no console errors. Firefox logs harmless WebGL notices.

## Known limitations

- Lakes aren't drawn as water yet: closed basins become flat plains.
- Rivers and roads you draw by hand don't carve the terrain.
- Territory borders don't snap together.
- Desktop only, and tested on macOS only so far. Windows and Linux reports are welcome.
