# Loremapper v1.2.0-alpha.1

Your planet as a globe, a polar map or a world map.

**Try it online:** <https://mercurial20.github.io/loremapper/>. Nothing to install, and your maps stay in your browser.

> An **alpha** of version 1.2: new features to try early. Please keep a backup
> (`Export → Editable project`) of maps you care about. This release doesn't change
> the project format, and maps from earlier versions open unchanged.

## See your planet in other projections

**View → Projection** shows a planet map, live:

| Projection | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Globe** | The planet as seen from space. Drag to turn it. |
| **Azimuthal (polar)** | Centred on the north or south pole, or anywhere: one hemisphere or the whole planet. Made for worlds that sit around a pole. |
| **Gott double-sided** | The northern and southern hemispheres as two disks that meet along the equator (Gott, Goldberg & Vanderbei, 2021), the least distorted flat map known. |
| **Equal Earth** | A modern atlas map with every area in true proportion. |
| **Mollweide** | The classic oval world map with true areas. |
| **Mercator** | True shapes and directions, with the poles stretched (cut at 85°). |

- Drag to turn the globe or move around; scroll or pinch to zoom. Phones and tablets work too.
- The **Info** tool and the status bar keep working: point at land to see its name, area and height.
- Projections are for looking. Your map's data never changes, and picking any editing tool brings back the editable equirectangular map.

## A mini globe in the corner

Planet maps now have a small globe in the corner. It shows:

- the whole world, with the part on screen outlined;
- the planet turning to follow the view, or seen from above the north or south pole.

It refreshes a moment after you edit. Click it to go somewhere, or hide it with one click.

## Export in any projection

**Export → Map image** offers the same six projections for planets, with:

- a live preview you can drag;
- terrain and the players' fog reprojected;
- rivers, roads, borders, symbols, labels and peaks drawn directly in the projection, so text stays upright and sharp;
- images up to 8192 px wide.

The export starts from whatever projection and centre you are viewing.

- The 19 desktop checks of the editor still pass in Chrome and WebKit; Firefox passes them too and logs only its usual WebGL notices.
