# Loremapper v1.3.1-alpha.3

A small release about rivers: their width, and how they look close up.

**Try it online:** <https://mercurial20.github.io/loremapper/>. Nothing to install, and your maps stay in your browser.

> An **alpha**: please keep a backup (`Export → Editable project`) of maps you care about.

## Changed

- **Rivers are drawn at a width on the map** (in metres or km, from the River tool's *Width on the map*), the same whatever the zoom you draw them at. A river drawn to join another starts narrower than the river it joins, so tributaries no longer come out as threads, or as wide as their river.
- **Rivers keep their width whatever joins them**, by default. Turn on *Widen below tributaries* in a river's panel to make it widen below each one; its slider then sets its width at the mouth, so a river with many tributaries can still be made thinner or wider.

## Fixed

- Rivers seen close up: no more spikes, barbs or notches where rivers join or bend, no gaps where a tributary meets its river, and thin rivers follow their bends and loops.
- Below a confluence a river widens gradually, not in a step.
- Rivers no longer loop back on themselves where their points are unevenly spaced.

## For older maps

- Rivers with tributaries are set to widen below them and keep the width they had at their mouth, so they look as they did. Turn *Widen below tributaries* off in a river's panel to keep it one width.
- The project format moves to version 7: maps saved by this release need 1.3.1-alpha.3 or later.

## Tested

- 167 unit tests, lint, type-check and the production build pass, among them a stress test of 40 random river networks (winding rivers of any width, tributaries joining at any angle, close up) checked against the rivers' true shapes, with no water drawn beside a river and none missing.
- In Chrome: rivers and tributaries drawn at different zooms; the width and *Widen below tributaries* in a river's panel; the map's drawing compared pixel by pixel with that check; generated rivers, river mouths and rivers from a source; 60 frames a second close up.
